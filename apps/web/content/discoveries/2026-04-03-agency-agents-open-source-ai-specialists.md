# agency-agents: 147 specialists is not the moat

Most agent demos still sell the fantasy.

One smart assistant. One prompt. One magic trick.

`agency-agents` goes the other direction. It's a giant open-source roster of specialist AI roles - frontend dev, SRE, copy chief, UX researcher, threat detection engineer, and a pile more. The GitHub repo is sitting north of 60K stars. That tells you the appetite is real.

## The core idea

The project packages AI work as named specialists instead of one blob assistant with mood swings.

Each agent has a job, a personality, and a rough lane. That's useful for one simple reason: humans already know how to think in roles. You don't ask your designer to do incident response. You don't ask your SRE to write launch copy. Role boundaries cut chaos.

That part matters. But it also means the role library itself is getting commoditized fast.

If anyone can download 147 agents in a few minutes, then "we have lots of agents" is not a business. It's a folder.

## Why it matters for Applied Leverage

This is the part people miss.

The hard problem is no longer creating more agent roles. The hard problem is getting a team of agents to do useful work without turning your company into a haunted house of half-finished tasks, duplicate output, and fake confidence.

That's why our stack keeps drifting toward things like Workgraph, Engram, acpx, Paperclip, and QA gates.

Role libraries create capability.

Operating systems create reliability.

If you're selling AI services to real businesses, that's the line that matters. The buyer doesn't care that you have 147 specialists. They care whether the right work gets assigned, tracked, reviewed, remembered, and shipped without the founder babysitting the whole mess.

## The catch

Big agent catalogs look impressive, but they can trick you into thinking specialization solved execution.

It didn't.

A larger roster can make the management problem worse. More roles means more handoffs, more overlap, more confusion about ownership, and more places for bad output to hide. The repo is valuable. The repo is not the system.

My read: projects like `agency-agents` are useful because they drag the market toward a new baseline. Good. Let them. Once specialist agents are cheap and public, the actual leverage moves upstream.

Into coordination.

Into memory.

Into governance.

Into proving the work was actually done.

That's the part worth building.
