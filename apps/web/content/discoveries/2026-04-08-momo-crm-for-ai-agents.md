# Momo: the CRM that gives your agents customer context

Most coding agents are still building half-blind.

They can read the repo. They can open tickets. They can spit out a PR. But most of them have no idea which users actually matter, who is quietly slipping away, or whether that shiny feature request came from a power user or just the loudest bastard in the room.

That's the hole Momo is going after.

The pitch is simple: give AI agents a CRM built from customer conversations and product analytics, then let that context shape what gets built next.

## The core idea

Momo connects tools like PostHog, Gmail, Fireflies, Google Calendar, Slack, and Notion. It pulls usage signals and conversation history into one customer profile.

That profile is the interesting part.

Most teams still keep CRM data in one place and product analytics in another, then wonder why the roadmap feels fuzzy. Momo tries to stitch the two together into something an operator or agent can use: who your power users are, which ICP-fit accounts are going quiet, what features top users keep asking for, and where churn signals are starting to show.

The sharpest line on the site is also the whole thesis: "Your agents know your code. Now they know your users."

If they can make that true in practice, that's a real shift. Most agent workflows stop at software context. Momo is trying to drag them one layer closer to the business.

## Why it matters for Applied Leverage

This hits a real problem in our stack.

A lot of agent systems can execute. Far fewer can judge. And the missing input usually is not another clever prompt. It's business context.

If an agent can see that your best customers hammer one workflow, that another segment drops off after a specific step, and that the same complaint keeps surfacing in calls and email, you stop shipping based on vibes.

That changes product work. It changes retention. It changes what kind of offers you build.

The bigger idea is simple: the next useful layer in agent infrastructure probably is not "more autonomous." It's "better grounded." Better memory. Better business signals. Better taste about what deserves action.

That's why this one is worth watching.

## The catch

This thing lives or dies on data quality and trust.

Merging CRM, analytics, email, meetings, docs, and team chatter sounds powerful. It also sounds like a great way to build a very confident machine on top of messy inputs, partial history, and bad sentiment reads.

There's a product risk too. Everybody says they are building a unified customer view. A depressing number of them are just building a bigger dashboard.

So yeah, the wedge is smart. The execution risk is brutal.

Still, I like the direction. Agents that can only see the codebase are junior. Agents that can see the customer might help you make better bets.
